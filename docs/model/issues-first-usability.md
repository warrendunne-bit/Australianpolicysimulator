# Housing pilot usability review

## First-time visitor sessions

Ask five people who have never used the simulator to take part. Include a mix of desktop and mobile users. Give each person the issue catalogue on a fresh visit with default settings. Ask them to think aloud for about ten minutes. Start with this prompt and let them choose where to go.

> Australia is considering faster home building and extra rent support during a downturn. Use the simulator to work out what changes for households and what governments would need to spend. Tell me what you think the results show as you go.

Ask the four questions in order. Read the follow-up only after the participant has interpreted the default result. If the participant gets stuck, record where and what they expected to happen before giving a hint.

| Question to ask | What an unaided answer should show |
| --- | --- |
| What does the baseline represent? | The model’s reference housing settings under the same chosen economic conditions as the two alternatives. |
| Who gets relief first and when do the first additional homes arrive? | The eligible example lower-income private renter gets a top-up in year one under the combined package. The first additional homes arrive in year three. |
| What does the least expensive intervention deliver, and what does the other package buy with the extra spending? | Faster supply has the lower additional public cost. Adding rental support further reduces the eligible renter’s housing burden. An answer can quote either annual or cumulative costs if it identifies the period correctly. |
| Which setting could you change, and what do you expect it to affect? | Names an actual control and gives a reasonable explanation, such as lower take-up reaches fewer eligible households and reduces aggregate spending. |

Ask the participant to change the setting they named, locate the updated comparison and explain what changed. If testing the worked example on a phone, observe whether the participant can read all three package columns and find years one, five and ten in the detailed results control.

Score each answer and the repeat task as unaided, assisted or unanswered. The planned threshold is four of five people answering all four questions and repeating the comparison unaided. If the threshold is missed, record the instructions or interface elements that caused difficulty, fix the clearest problems and repeat with new visitors.

## Internal review findings

- The worked example answers a fixed year-five question alongside controls for ten years of detailed results. The year control now explains which results it changes, and its explanation is associated programmatically with the control.
- The target row now spells out whether the package meets the illustrative three-percentage-point goal. The worked example invites visitors to change one assumption, rental support take-up, and presents modelled recipient counts alongside the example household’s costs and cumulative public spending.
- The default case shows earlier relief for the eligible renter, construction beginning to deliver additional homes in year three, and higher government spending for the package that adds rental support. The brief uses gross income for the observed Australian context and disposable income for the model’s household examples.
- Component rendering verified the fixed June 2030 comparison while the detailed year took the values June 2026, June 2030 and June 2035. Lowering take-up from 80% to 60% reduced the combined package’s modelled year-five recipients from 733,899 to 550,424 and additional spending through year five from A$19,107 million to A$16,330 million in nominal terms. The participating renter’s year-five housing burden remained 42.8% of disposable income.

First-time visitor sessions are pending. The internal review checks the route, labels and calculations. The sessions will show whether visitors can interpret and repeat the comparison unaided.
